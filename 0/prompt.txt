## 2. Key Technical Concepts

- **Behavior-as-writer vs Behavior-as-reaction.** A Behavior that *performs* a write can enforce (atomic, in-tx). A *reaction* cannot — post-commit, read-only sandbox (`manage_entity.ts` comment at `queue()`; `sandbox/client-sdk.ts:263` forces `SET TRANSACTION READ ONLY`). A reaction can compensate, never refuse.
- **Cross-output atomicity — PROVEN.** `complete-window.ts:841` promotes every declared output in ONE loop against ONE `tx` inside `sql.begin` (`handleCompleteWindow` at `:601`). A throw in output B rolls back output A.
- **`runMutationGate` runs inside `sql.begin`** with `sql: tx`, holding the row's `FOR UPDATE` lock (`entity-management.ts:696`).
- **`entity-hooks.ts` has only `beforeCreate`/`afterCreate`/`beforeDelete`** — no update hook, no tx in context.
- **Three write-path gaps proven in prod:** a declared `default` does NOT satisfy `required` on create; `entities.update` validates the PATCH not the merged result; a soft-deleted entity still holds its name.
- **NEW: entity name uniqueness is ORG-WIDE, not per `entity_type`.** Proved by probe.
- **NEW: writing `null` into an `integer` property stores `0`**, not null.
